# Sprint 27 — Executive Summary

**The question this sprint set out to answer — does Aditya-L1 carry measurable predictive value beyond GOES? — turns out to be a question the repository has never fairly asked.** Every prior "no" is compromised by the test conditions, and every prior "maybe" is compromised by the evidence base. The synthesis across the seven analyses is that the Aditya-L1 instruments were given a test no instrument could pass: their data enters the model as 22 raw, unnormalized columns of which 12 are near-duplicates of the other 10 (rate-versus-counts correlations 0.85–0.96, HEL1OS band-to-band 0.9911), with none of the engineered temporal features that the repository's own information-gap audit proved carry essentially all of GOES's skill; a quarter of SoLEXS minutes arrive as silent zero-fill because per-minute availability masks are collapsed to a single label-minute scalar at loading; the branch encoders — the largest in the model — received gradient updates on only eighteen months of real data against sixteen years for GOES; and the corpus used to audit instrument-target relationships contains zero M/X flares, making its universal 0.0 correlations an arithmetic tautology rather than a finding.

Three consequences follow, in order of decision weight. **First, the V4 direction question (fusion versus architecture) is not yet decidable, and deciding it now would be premature on either side.** The strongest instrument-negative evidence — conditional mutual information of 0.0 given a five-feature GOES history baseline, and a single-seed model ablation where adding SoLEXS *decreased* True Skill Score — was generated against defective inputs; the strongest instrument-positive evidence — significant marginal delta-AUC for the soft-band compression — is surrogate-task-only and vanishes under conditioning. **Second, the cheapest untested idea in the project is not an Aditya-L1 idea at all:** the GOES two-channel ratio has never been formed into the standard isothermal temperature and emission-measure features (the White, Thomas & Schwartz 2005 inversion), testable on the full sixteen-year archive with the existing, measured V1 pipeline in about ninety minutes. If that arm alone recovers meaningful skill, the "feature engineering over instrument addition" conclusion is effectively made. **Third, the decisive experiment is now specified and affordable:** physics-engineered, deduplicated, log-scaled Aditya features (spectral hardness dynamics, Neupert fluence integrals, non-thermal-to-thermal ratios) against the current raw-feature fusion and against GOES-only, on the flare-bearing Stage-2 splits — roughly 17 to 26 single-seed hours on the measured Apple M4 baseline, two to three overnights, with the fusion-architecture variant explicitly deferred to CUDA because the fusion layer is demonstrably not the binding constraint.

The requirements analysis binds this into Version 4 governance: five mandatory requirements (frozen-harness matched-baseline evaluation, multi-seed evidence, provenance gating, flare-bearing joint evidence before any fusion claim, and fair inputs before any instrument verdict) ensure V4 cannot repeat the two failure patterns that consumed Version 3 — optimizing against unproven yardsticks, and drawing instrument conclusions from tests the instruments could not have passed. The recommended next step is the two-arm decisive slice of the campaign (GOES temperature/emission-measure features, and the fair Aditya feature test), because those two results — not another architecture debate — determine whether Version 4 is a feature-engineering program, a fusion program, or an architecture-redesign program.
