# Sprint 28 — Executive Plan (Task 8)

**Proceed with Version 4 implementation — because the plan's first two sprints cost about three weeks and 10–12 compute-hours, and they resolve the one question every subsequent architecture dollar depends on: whether the project's remaining headroom lives in physics-engineered features, in the Aditya-L1 instruments, or in neither.**

## What a decision-maker needs to know

**The situation.** Version 3 is frozen, honest, and modest: it beats persistence by +0.0794 True Skill Score with a working provenance system, but Sprint 26 proved the training procedure is exhausted, and Sprint 27 proved the flagship Aditya-L1 instruments were never fairly tested — their data reaches the model as duplicated, unnormalized, dynamics-free raw channels, and the corpus used to audit their value contains zero M/X flares. The project therefore faces a three-way fork (features, fusion, or architecture redesign), and Version 4's defining property is that it buys the answer *before* committing to a branch, rather than guessing.

**What Version 4 actually is.** Not an architecture. It is: (1) a rebuilt feature space — 32 inputs replacing 36, dropping twelve redundant columns and adding seventeen physics-derived features, each tied to a named solar-physics principle with a per-feature validation test; (2) a rebuilt dataset pipeline that stops feeding the model fabricated zeros and label-minute masks, with train-only normalization and dataset-level provenance manifests; (3) a pre-registered four-arm experiment whose outcome mechanically selects the architecture via a decision tree with pre-committed tie-breaking; and (4) an operator decision layer (functioning RED tier, duty-cycle targets) that proceeds regardless of which arm wins, because operator trust was the stated goal and the current 41.5%-of-the-time yellow alerting fails it independently of model skill.

**What it costs.** Seven to nine calendar weeks and roughly 25–45 Metal Performance Shaders compute-hours end to end, of which the decision-resolving core (Sprints 29–31) is about 4.5 weeks and 15–22 hours — all calibrated to measured Sprint 26A timings, not guesses. The recorded project deadline (2026-12-31, `PROJECT_CONFIG.yaml`) leaves a wide margin; the hackathon date itself is not recorded in the repository, so explicit cut lines exist down to a 4-week floor that ships the fair-test verdict, the decision layer, and the scientific-integrity story without claiming a new model.

**What could go wrong, ranked.** First, the fair test may be statistically underpowered: the pre-registered +0.02 minimum effect sits against a seed-variance band no one has measured — the highest-risk unresolved criticism, mitigated by a pre-registered escalation to five seeds if the first three-seed spread exceeds 0.015. Second, the most likely scientific outcome (assessed ~40%) is that Aditya-L1 shows no value even under fair conditions — which is a publishable negative and redirects effort to the GOES-physics path, but ends the multi-instrument story the mission narrative prefers; the plan treats that honestly rather than protecting the narrative. Third, the "neither improves" branch (~35%) would leave architecture redesign as the only skill lever, with genuinely uncertain payoff — in that world the operator-decision-layer sprint is the insurance policy, because it improves the deployed system's trustworthiness with zero dependence on model gains. Fourth, external credibility: no community benchmark (NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center forecasts) exists in-repository; acquiring that archive is the single highest-value addition for publication and is flagged as the standing unresolved criticism U2.

**Why proceed rather than wait.** Every alternative is worse: skipping to architecture redesign ignores that the cheapest untested physics (the GOES temperature/emission-measure inversion — three features, one measured-scale training run) has never been tried; skipping to fusion engineering repeats Version 3's mistake of building sophistication on inputs proven defective; and doing nothing leaves a submission whose strongest material — the leakage forensics, the pre-registration discipline, the honest baselines — is already in hand but whose scientific ceiling is a +0.08 margin over persistence. The first Version 4 sprint is also deliberately the safest: version control, tests, and provenance land before any pipeline is touched.

**The one decision being made now.** Approving Sprints 29–31 (non-negotiable core). Sprint 32's content is decided by the experiment, not by this document — that is the point of the design.
