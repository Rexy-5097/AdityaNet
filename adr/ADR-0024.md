# ADR-0024 — Supersession and erratum

## Context

Immutable releases with no retraction path leave a platform unable to respond when a parser
defect invalidates published work. Scientific publishing has had retractions for a century;
an integrity platform cannot have fewer tools than a journal.

## Decision

**Supersession** is a first-class, immutable record: the affected release digest, the
superseding digest or null, a `severity` of `CORRECTION` | `RETRACTION` | `DEPRECATION`, a
reason, an effective date, and the provenance of the finding.

Invariants:

1. The bytes of a superseded release **never change and are never deleted**.
2. Supersession records are themselves immutable and content-addressed.
3. The evidence gate surfaces supersession **transitively**: any Claim whose provenance DAG
   reaches a superseded release renders a notice. A `RETRACTION` anywhere in the DAG
   **fails the build**.
4. Evaluations against superseded inputs remain valid as historical record and are labelled
   as such. Deleting them would destroy the audit trail justifying the retraction.

## Consequences

- Immutability of content is preserved absolutely; only standing changes.
- A retraction can cascade to many published claims, by design.

## Verification

PROP and INTG per VVMP §2.1: a no-mutation property; an integration test in which a retracted
release three hops upstream fails the build.
